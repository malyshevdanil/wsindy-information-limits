# Recent weak estimation and test design: scope audit

Search and primary-source review: 11–12 October 2026. Published metadata for the two journal/book items is checked against publisher-deposited Crossref records; the two remaining items are identified as preprints. Full author texts were read through their arXiv HTML versions. These notes distinguish existing methods from the specific theorem in this manuscript.

The older continuous-regression result of Dette, Pepelyshev and Zhigljavsky remains the direct precedent for optimal signed measures with endpoint masses. Original WSINDy and WENDy already use covariance-aware weak regression and test selection. The novelty statement is restricted to the adjoint-range characterization, its sharp information loss, and the explicit OU construction with conditional sequential small-noise efficiency.

Publication status: Rummel and Chen are cited as the inspected arXiv versions; no unverified journal placement is assigned. Tran was published online on 28 July 2026. Chawla's final chapter metadata gives 2026 and pages 1639–1746, superseding the 2025 preprint date in the bibliography.

## Statistical ODE and Gaussian background audit

Brunel, Clairon and d'Alche-Buc (2014), Section 2.3 of https://arxiv.org/html/1410.7566, already identify boundary information loss and adapt tests to known initial values; their GMM weighting is an antecedent. Heitzman-Breen, Dukic and Bortz (2026), Discussion in https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-026-01639-x, also state the loss of initial-state information. Both primary texts were inspected. Neither inspected text supplies the correlated fixed-horizon adjoint-range and Schur-complement theorem proved here. This is a scoped literature comparison, not a claim of exhaustive priority.

Qi and Zhao (2010), Theorem 3.3, https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.3400: efficiency of generalized profiling requires their iid observation assumptions, increasing penalty and controlled approximation. Dattner and Klaassen (2015), https://arxiv.org/html/1305.4126: root-n direct integral estimation; one-step refinement is discussed rather than proved there. The one-step asymptotic-equivalence result is Dattner and Gugushvili (2018), Theorem 1, https://arxiv.org/html/1503.07973. These full author texts were inspected; none is cited as proving the present physical-OU experiment.

Brunel (2008), https://arxiv.org/pdf/0710.4190, and Gugushvili and Klaassen (2012), https://arxiv.org/html/1007.3880, were checked in full author versions for smoothing/gradient matching and root-n assertions. Varah (1982), https://doi.org/10.1137/0903003, and Liang and Wu (2008), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19956350/, were checked at primary publisher/author abstract level only; manuscript claims are limited to spline fitting and measurement-error regression methods.

Parzen (1961), https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177704840: bibliographic metadata verified; full article access unsuccessful. Ibragimov and Has'minskii (1981), https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4899-0027-2, and Kutoyants (1994), https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-011-1020-4, were checked through publisher descriptions/contents, not read as complete books. They are general background citations, not sources for a specific new theorem or for identical observation assumptions. Kutoyants concerns small-noise dynamical/diffusion estimation rather than this additive stationary measurement-error setup.

Zhang (2004), primary author copy https://www.stat.purdue.edu/~zhanghao/Paper/JASA2004.pdf, Introduction and Theorem 2: the exponential-covariance microergodic combination is variance divided by range. Hence the fitted-shape sensitivity check does not imply separate infill consistency of tau and sigma squared on one fixed interval.

WENDy-MLE also includes a weak-to-output-error hybrid (Section 3.2.2) and an iid-noise Lorenz demonstration (Section 3.5). The present likelihood-refinement comparison and third-system check are evidence for the correlated-noise information reference, not new hybrid or Lorenz-estimation methods.
