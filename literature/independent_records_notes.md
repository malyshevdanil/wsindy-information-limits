# Independent-record GMM: literature and scope

Checked on 12 October 2026. Hansen (1982), DOI 10.2307/1912775, supplies the established GMM consistency, normality and optimal-weight framework. Author-hosted full paper: https://larspeterhansen.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Hansen-econometrica-GMM.pdf . The independent-record result here specializes this framework to exactly corrected polynomial weak moments; it is not a new generic GMM theorem.

Newey and McFadden (1994), Handbook of Econometrics IV, chapter 36, pp. 2111-2245, DOI 10.1016/S1573-4412(05)80005-4. Full chapter from an academic host: https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~xshi/Newey_Mcfadden_handbook.pdf . Sections 3.3, 4.3 and 5.2 cover GMM normality, variance and optimal weights. Sections 6.1-6.3 and Theorem 6.1 explicitly account for the first-stage influence in two-step standard errors. These portions were inspected; the manuscript uses this established correction rather than treating estimated Hermite variance as fixed.

Brunel, Clairon and d'Alche-Buc (2014), DOI 10.1080/01621459.2013.841583, author full text https://arxiv.org/html/1410.7566 . Their orthogonality conditions, GMM weighting and qualitative boundary-information observation are precedents. Their setting uses a nonparametric proxy and independent observation errors; their formulation also treats delay equations. Our fixed-horizon OU adjoint geometry and repeated independent records answer complementary questions. No strict dominance or general first-GMM claim is appropriate.

Necessary qualifications: exact Hermite moments do not remove numerical integration defects; a fixed grid can retain a deterministic pseudo-target. Full residual covariance is optimal for known correction parameters in a fixed family. A same-data fitted correction variance contributes a nuisance influence and its cross-covariance, so unknown-covariance optimal weights use the resulting effective covariance. Independent-copy OU identification requires shared deterministic mean for the pair-difference construction. Stacking can be inconsistent when the corrected design remains correlated with its residual, but exceptions exist. Small-noise efficiency, fixed-noise record asymptotics and single-path infill are three distinct experiments.
