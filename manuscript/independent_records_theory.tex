\section{Fixed-noise weak GMM across independent
records}\label{fixed-noise-weak-gmm-across-independent-records}

\subsection{S14. Common-trajectory GMM reference and
proof}\label{s14.-common-trajectory-gmm-reference-and-proof}

The replicate asymptotic is distinct from single-path infill and from
Corollary 1. Let \(Y_r=x_{\theta_0,u}+\eta_r\), \(r=1,\ldots,n\), be
independent copies on the same fixed interval. The deterministic
trajectory and initial state are shared, the covariance is stationary
Gaussian, and its amplitude is any fixed positive finite value. Assume a
supplied polynomial vector field linear in \(\theta\in\mathbb R^p\), a
fixed collection of boundary-vanishing tests, and exact integrals. Write
the Hermite-corrected weak moment as

\[
m_r(\theta,\nu)=b_r-G_r(\nu)\theta\in\mathbb R^M,
\qquad D=E G_r(\nu_0),\qquad E m_r(\theta_0,\nu_0)=0,
\]

where \(\nu\) contains the marginal variances used in the correction and
any covariance-shape parameters. The expectation identity follows by the
Gaussian heat/Hermite identity at every time, Fubini, and the
deterministic weak ODE identity. It is exact at fixed noise; it does not
assert that the ratio or least-squares estimator from one record is
unbiased.

\textbf{Lemma S14.1 (fixed-noise independent-record GMM).} Suppose \(D\)
has column rank \(p\), the covariance
\(\Omega=\operatorname{Cov}(m_r(\theta_0,\nu_0))\) is positive definite,
and moments have finite second moments. With known \(\nu_0\) and random
positive-definite \(W_n\to_p W\), define

\[
\widehat\theta_n=(\bar G_n^\top W_n\bar G_n)^{-1}\bar G_n^\top W_n\bar b_n,
\qquad P_W=(D^\top W D)^{-1}D^\top W.
\]

Then \(\widehat\theta_n\to_p\theta_0\) and

\[
\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_0)\Rightarrow N(0,V_W),
\qquad V_W=P_W\Omega P_W^\top.
\]

Within this fixed moment family and the corresponding regular linear GMM
influence class, \(W=\Omega^{-1}\) minimizes covariance, giving
\(V_*=(D^\top\Omega^{-1}D)^{-1}\). For polynomial moments, \(\Omega\) is
the full covariance from Proposition 3, including every nonzero Hermite
order. Empirical residual covariance at a consistent preliminary
estimate, or a consistent full model-based covariance, supplies feasible
two-step weights. First-order covariance alone generally gives a
different, nonoptimal \(V_W\) at fixed noise. The theorem does not
assert full-path likelihood efficiency at finite amplitude.

For estimated \(\nu\), additionally assume local differentiability and
an asymptotically linear nuisance estimator

\[
\sqrt n(\widehat\nu-\nu_0)=n^{-1/2}\sum_{r=1}^n\xi_r+o_p(1),
\quad E\xi_r=0,\quad E\|\xi_r\|^2<\infty,
\quad H=E\partial_\nu m_r(\theta_0,\nu_0).
\]

Assume the corresponding sample derivative converges locally, with a
uniform second-order Taylor remainder \(o_p(n^{-1/2})\). Then the same
closed-form estimator, evaluated at \(\widehat\nu\), is consistent and
asymptotically normal with

\[
\psi_r=m_r(\theta_0,\nu_0)+H\xi_r,
\quad \widetilde\Omega=\operatorname{Cov}(\psi_r),
\quad \widetilde V_W=P_W\widetilde\Omega P_W^\top.
\]

If \(\widetilde\Omega\) is positive definite, estimated inverse
\(\widetilde\Omega\) is optimal \textbf{for this specified nuisance
estimator and moment influence family}. Its optimum is not generally the
known-covariance optimum or the globally optimal joint GMM estimator.
Estimating only a shape parameter that changes weights but not mean
correction has zero \(H\) for that parameter; estimating a variance used
in Hermite correction need not. A consistent empirical influence
covariance, including cross-covariances between \(m_r\) and \(\xi_r\),
yields marginal Wald intervals and joint ellipsoids with asymptotic
nominal coverage. These are standard GMM and two-step-estimation
arguments specialized to the weak Hermite moments {[}1, 2{]}.

\emph{Proof.} Polynomial Gaussian functionals on a bounded interval with
continuous deterministic mean have moments of all finite orders; bounded
test functions allow the required exchanges of integration and
expectation. For known covariance, the law of large numbers gives
\(\bar G_n\to D\), \(\bar b_n\to D\theta_0\), and convergence of the
inverse normal matrix. The exact identity

\[
\widehat\theta_n-\theta_0=P_n\bar m_n(\theta_0,\nu_0)
\]

and the iid multivariate CLT give the stated limit, without a
small-noise expansion. If \(P D=I_p\), set
\(P_*= (D^\top\Omega^{-1}D)^{-1}D^\top\Omega^{-1}\). Then

\[
P\Omega P^\top-P_*\Omega P_*^\top
=(P-P_*)\Omega(P-P_*)^\top\succeq0,
\]

because the cross terms vanish. A consistent preliminary least-squares
estimate makes the empirical covariance converge to \(\Omega\) by the
law of large numbers and polynomial continuity. In the nuisance case,
the stipulated Taylor expansion gives

\[
\sqrt n\bar m_n(\theta_0,\widehat\nu)
=n^{-1/2}\sum_r[m_r(\theta_0,\nu_0)+H\xi_r]+o_p(1).
\]

The random design and random weights multiply a \(O_p(n^{-1/2})\)
centered mean, so their consistency suffices for the leading map. Apply
the joint CLT to \((m_r,\xi_r)\) and the same covariance decomposition
using \(\widetilde\Omega\). Consistency of the sandwich and coverage
follow by Slutsky. This proves the assertions.

\textbf{Numerical quadrature condition.} On a fixed discrete grid,
Hermite correction is still unbiased for the corresponding
\emph{quadrature moment}. Its deterministic expectation is the weak
quadrature defect, which is generally nonzero at \(\theta_0\).
Consistency for the continuous ODE therefore requires exact integration
or vanishing defect. In a triangular array, require
\(\sqrt n\|E m_{r,h_n}(\theta_0,\nu_0)\|\to0\), convergence of designs
and covariance, and uniform integrability sufficient for the triangular
CLT and nuisance remainder. Mean-square convergence of quadrature
moments to their continuous versions gives the stochastic part for this
fixed test family. This condition concerns grid refinement with
independent records; it does not prove a joint
growing-window/small-noise rate. The numerical experiment uses a fixed
fine grid and discloses its clean defect rather than claiming literal
fixed-grid consistency for the ODE parameter.

\subsubsection{S14.1. Consistent OU shape from independent
copies}\label{s14.1.-consistent-ou-shape-from-independent-copies}

For even \(n=2B\), define independent pairs
\(d_r=(Y_{2r-1}-Y_{2r})/\sqrt2\). Their common deterministic mean
cancels and \(d_r\) has the original stationary OU covariance. At a
fixed nondegenerate grid \(t_0,\ldots,t_L\) with mesh \(h>0\), let

\[
s_{rj}=\frac1{L+1}\sum_{i=0}^L d_{rj}(t_i)^2,
\quad a_r=\sum_{j,i<L}d_{rj}(t_i)d_{rj}(t_{i+1}),
\quad c_r=\sum_{j,i<L}d_{rj}(t_i)^2.
\]

Then \(\widehat v_j=B^{-1}\sum_r s_{rj}\to v_j\),
\(\widehat\rho=\bar a/\bar c\to e^{-h/\tau}\), and
\(\widehat\tau=-h/\log\widehat\rho\to\tau\). Their joint \(\sqrt B\)
normality follows from the independent-pair CLT and the delta method;
dependence of time points is retained inside each pair. Positivity of
variances and \(0<\rho<1\) make the transformation regular. The
influence for \(v_j\) is \(s_{rj}-v_j\), and for \(\rho\) it is
\((a_r-\rho c_r)/E c_r\); the \(\tau\) influence is
\(h\xi_{\rho,r}/[\rho(\log\rho)^2]\). If finite-sample clipping is
needed, any fixed bounds containing the true parameters in their
interior are asymptotically inactive and must be reported.

Use pair-averaged weak moments for inference, so the theorem applies
with sample size \(B\), independent pair influences, and variance
divided by \(B=n/2\). In Van der Pol the only correction derivative is
\(H_{v_1,2k}=\theta_4 E\int\phi_k Y_2\); the other rows and
variance/shape derivatives are zero. Thus the empirical covariance of
pair residuals plus this derivative times \((s_{r1}-\widehat v_1)\)
supplies the nuisance-adjusted weights and sandwich. Averaging pair
differences for shape estimation requires a common deterministic mean;
heterogeneous initial conditions would require a different construction.
Replication permits separate OU variance and range consistency that is
unavailable under fixed-horizon infill of one observed path.

\subsubsection{S14.2. Why stacking can retain
bias}\label{s14.2.-why-stacking-can-retain-bias}

Stacked corrected least squares converges, when its normal matrix is
nonsingular, to

\[
\theta_{\mathrm{stack}}=\theta_0+
\{E(G_r^\top G_r)\}^{-1}E[G_r^\top m_r(\theta_0,\nu_0)].
\]

Exact \(E m_r=0\) does not imply \(E[G_r^\top m_r]=0\), since the design
and residual share measurement error. Stacking is therefore generally
biased and inconsistent in this errors-in-variables setting; special
orthogonality exceptions are possible. Averaged-moment GMM instead uses
\(\bar G_n^\top W_n\bar m_n\), whose probability limit is zero at the
true parameter. The stacking behavior in Table S11 is an instance of
this distinction, not a claim that every stacked regression must be
inconsistent.

With known covariance, the same argument extends to iid random initial
states sharing the coefficient vector, provided the conditional weak
identity, full-rank mean design and finite moments hold. This covers the
mechanism in the heterogeneous-initial-state aggregation example. The
pair-difference nuisance estimator above still requires a common
deterministic mean and is not applied to that heterogeneous example.

\subsubsection{S14.3. Misspecified temporal
weighting}\label{s14.3.-misspecified-temporal-weighting}

For known marginal correction and any nonsingular assumed temporal
shape, the fixed-family variance is the sandwich
\(P_W\Omega_{\mathrm{true}}P_W^\top/n\). In the small-noise
single-record setting replace \(\Omega_{\mathrm{true}}\) with the
order-one covariance and the factor \(1/n\) with \(\epsilon^2\). Correct
marginal correction preserves averaged-moment consistency even if
\(\tau\) is wrong; a wrong variance used for Hermite correction can
destroy the moment identity. The finite-noise fitted-shape rows in S12
change both correction and weights and cannot be read as a pure
temporal-weighting experiment. The \(\tau/2\) and \(2\tau\) rows retain
the true marginal variances.
