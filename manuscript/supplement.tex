\subsection{S1. Continuous and discrete
likelihood}\label{s1.-continuous-and-discrete-likelihood}

All parameters share known positive stationary OU covariance. The
innovation transformation separates an independent normal initial
observation from a Brownian drift experiment. The translation theorem
{[}1{]} suffices for this common-covariance case; a changing-covariance
criterion {[}2{]} is unnecessary. The family-wide consistency
obstruction is classical {[}3{]}. Unknown initial conditions would enter
the sensitivity matrix as nuisance parameters.

For the exponential model, set \(q_a(t)=x_0(a+\tau^{-1})e^{at}\) and
\(\nu^2=2\sigma^2/\tau\). Continuous likelihood is

\[
\log L(a;Y)=\nu^{-2}\left[\int_0^Tq_a(t)(dY(t)+Y(t)dt/\tau)-\frac12\int_0^Tq_a(t)^2dt\right]+\text{constant}.
\]

The initial term is independent of \(a\) when \(x_0\) is fixed. The
exact discrete negative log likelihood, with \(e_n(a)=y_n-x_0e^{at_n}\),
is

\[
-\log L_h(a)=\frac{e_0(a)^2}{2\sigma^2}+
\frac{\sum_{n=1}^N[e_n(a)-\rho e_{n-1}(a)]^2}{2\sigma^2(1-\rho^2)}+\text{constant}.
\]

The independent validation constrains optimization to \([-2.5,0.5]\)
before simulation. Sixty-one parameter-grid points identify candidate
local minima, each refined by bounded scalar optimization; endpoints are
checked. This is a numerical bounded-likelihood estimate, without a
proof that all minima between coarse points are found. It need not be
unbiased or attain the Cramér--Rao bound. Its squared error is finite by
boundedness. The weak ratio is summarized by quantiles rather than a
population MSE.

The separate geometry execution performs deterministic sensitivity
quadrature for the scalar example. It computes the joint information for
\((a,x_0)\), its Schur complement and the weak influence's normalization
and initial-state annihilation. Both identities are verified to
\(10^{-9}\) on the finest reference grid. Its config, source fingerprint
and output archive are retained; this computation adds no stochastic
paths and is not an additional Monte Carlo replication.

\subsection{S2. Mixed-noise
covariance}\label{s2.-mixed-noise-covariance}

Let each sample error contain OU variance \(v_{c,j}\) and independent
nugget variance \(v_{w,j}\), with total \(v_j=v_{c,j}+v_{w,j}\). For
assumed correction \(\widehat v\), use
\(\bar f=\exp[\frac12\sum_j(v_j-\widehat v_j)\partial_j^2]f\) in the
residual coefficients. The exact discrete covariance is

\[
\Sigma_r=\sum_{\alpha\ne0}\alpha!\left[
v_c^\alpha U_\alpha^TR_{|\alpha|,h}U_\alpha+
(v^\alpha-v_c^\alpha)U_\alpha^TU_\alpha\right].
\]

Distinct times share only OU covariance; equal times contain total
marginal variance. The response--library covariance is included in
\(U_\alpha\). Correctness fixtures compare this expression with Gaussian
quadrature, including raw and miscalibrated features.

\subsection{S3. Covariance-estimation Monte Carlo
error}\label{s3.-covariance-estimation-monte-carlo-error}

For independent centered vectors \(r_m\) with covariance \(\Sigma\), use
\(S_n=n^{-1}\sum_mr_mr_m^T\) and theoretical centering. Independence
gives

\[
E\|S_n-\Sigma\|_F^2=\frac{E\|r\|^4-\|\Sigma\|_F^2}{n}.
\]

For Gaussian vectors
\(E\|r\|^4=(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2+2\|\Sigma\|_F^2\), making the
relative RMS floor

\[
\frac{\sqrt{(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2+\|\Sigma\|_F^2}}{\sqrt n\,\|\Sigma\|_F}.
\]

Polynomial residuals above order one are non-Gaussian. The protocol
estimates their fourth moment and labels the Gaussian expression as a
reference. An affine-null bootstrap centers archived residuals, whitens
empirical covariance, recolors to the analytical target and resamples
250 multinomial count vectors with the original path count. Covariance
eigenvalues are floored at \(10^{-12}\) of the largest during whitening.
This approximate null retains empirical shape; its exceedance fraction
is not an exact test p-value.

Coefficient validation uses 2,000 paths at amplitudes
\(0.001,0.01,0.05,0.2\). Errors subtract the clean discrete fit. The
residual surrogate uses the true coefficients; the exact finite-grid
derivative about the clean fit would instead use its slightly
quadrature-shifted coefficients. This remaining quadrature-scale
distinction is retained rather than described as an exact finite-grid
coefficient identity. A guard requires every support-restricted design's
smallest singular value to exceed half the clean value. Actual
covariance is conditional on this event, whereas the linearized Gaussian
covariance is unguarded. Counts, bias and paired linearization errors
accompany discrepancy values. The protocol does not presume finite
unconditional nonlinear estimator moments.

\subsection{S4. Protocol, implementation and
provenance}\label{s4.-protocol-implementation-and-provenance}

\texttt{configs/revision.json} was fixed before the corresponding
numerical outputs; it is not a public preregistration. Numerical
experiments execute in CPU Kaggle notebooks. Batched deterministic Van
der Pol integration uses the recorded solver and tolerances; a
correctness check compares batched and individual trajectories.
Duplicate executions are not counted as independent evidence. Six
synthetic residual arrays are used for covariance-error calibration.
Notebook source fingerprints identify the executed implementation; local
manuscript edits do not regenerate those execution snapshots.

Seed ranges start at 1,000,000 for sparse regression, 1,100,000 for
scalar likelihood, 1,200,000 for coefficient validation, 1,300,000 for
covariance bootstrap and 1,500,000 for heterogeneous records. Other
experiment ranges are disjoint. Seed reuse within a condition pairs
grids and methods. Conditions use common random numbers and cannot be
pooled as independent experiments.

Four systems, two regimes, two amplitudes, four grids, three methods and
128 paths produce 24,576 fits. Thirty fourth-power bump functions have
full support width 1.6 for decay and three for oscillators. All centers
remain fixed. The correlation ratio is \(\tau/L=0.1\). The independent
protocol has no forecasting endpoint. Scalar likelihood has 1,000 paths
on six nested grids. Heterogeneous acquisition uses 400 blocks with up
to 16 recordings, initial offsets uniform on \([-0.25,0.25]^2\) around
\((2,0)\) and fixed sensor noise amplitudes derived from the baseline
path.

Relative MSTLS uses zero ridge penalty, unit rescaling, 50 logarithmic
thresholds \(10^{-4}\) to one and loss weight 0.8. Seven equation
fixtures matched the pinned author regression code exactly; the
repository commit and SHA-256 are recorded in the parity report. This
component does not reproduce adaptive WSINDy or simultaneous noise
reconstruction in WmSINDy {[}4, 5{]}. Author source is inspected
separately and excluded from the distribution package because no
redistribution license was found in the inspected tree.

Every output retains source fingerprint, execution environment and
completion status. Completed notebooks remain immutable snapshots. Seven
correctness tests check exponential covariance application, integration
by parts, OU sampling, Hermite correction, exact raw/miscalibrated/mixed
covariance, clean recovery and conservative diagnostic refusal.
Additional checks verify common-variance scaling of IRLS and batched
versus individual ODE integration. Local postprocessing creates tables
and figures from downloaded results. Median intervals resample
independent paths; support intervals are Wilson intervals. No global
multiplicity-adjusted comparison is claimed.

\subsection{S5. Aggregation and approximation
diagnostics}\label{s5.-aggregation-and-approximation-diagnostics}

Coefficient-level aggregation diagnostics use the same 400 blocks,
random seeds, initial conditions and sensor errors as Table S5. A
separate execution preserves the coefficient matrices without changing
the estimators. It replays the existing experiment and is not an
additional set of independent records. Error norms are checked against
the original output before combining these results.

For coefficient error vectors \(e_m\) normalized by \(\|\xi^*\|_F\),
define \(B=\|\bar e\|\), \(S^2=n^{-1}\sum_m\|e_m-\bar e\|^2\) and
\(R^2=n^{-1}\sum_m\|e_m\|^2\). The empirical identity \(R^2=B^2+S^2\)
separates squared bias of the sample mean from dispersion; finite-sample
\(B^2\) includes sampling error of that mean. An adjusted estimate
subtracts \(\operatorname{tr}(\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}e)/n\) using
the unbiased sample covariance. Medians alone do not identify this
decomposition.

The relative RMS linearization diagnostic in Table S4 divides the RMS
difference between actual and linearized coefficient displacements by
the RMS linear displacement. It can exceed one. All 2000 paths pass the
singular-value guard at each amplitude, so guarded and complete
empirical samples coincide; this is not proof of finite population
moments.

\subsection{S6. Complete adaptive author-pipeline
reproduction}\label{s6.-complete-adaptive-author-pipeline-reproduction}

A separate protocol, \texttt{configs/full\_adaptive.json}, was frozen
before comparative outputs. It uses Van der Pol with parameter one,
initial state (2,0), duration 20, noise amplitude five percent, OU
correlation time 0.3 or independent errors, and meshes 0.04 and 0.01.
Seeds 910000--910015 give 16 independent records per cell. All
algorithms receive the same fine-grid error realization and its coarse
restriction; SHA256 checks verify paired inputs. This seed range is
separate from the earlier studies. Discovery libraries have nine
nonconstant monomials of total degree one through three, matching the
released WmSINDy example. Fixed-window discovery and supplied-support
comparators are rerun on that library, rather than reusing ten-feature
results.

Pinned author repositories are WSINDy\_ODE commit \texttt{72d8d6e5be2b},
WENDy commit \texttt{65c52a893b1f}, and the WmSINDy Van der Pol example
at commit \texttt{f1a781009a5c} {[}4--6{]}. Files or repository ZIPs are
fetched from those revisions and verified with the full revision
identifiers and SHA256 values in \texttt{configs/author\_pins.json}.
Core author functions are executed without rewriting the statistical
algorithms. Author sources are excluded from distributed archives;
download instructions and our adapters are provided.

WSINDy uses FFT-corner widths and polynomial degrees, gradient-adapted
placement with relax\_AG=1, automatic SMAF smoothing initialized as in
the released driver, scaling parameter two, 100 thresholds from 1e-4 to
one, zero ridge penalty and loss weight 0.8. The adaptive placement is
explicitly enabled relative to the released driver's uniform-placement
default. GLS remains disabled, as in that driver. Selected widths,
degrees, centers, smoothing-filter lengths and losses are saved.

WENDy uses the released set-and-forget settings: estimated noise, the
exp(-9/(1-x²)) bump, automatic minimum radius and four dyadic multiples,
SVD corner truncation, maximum 100 iterations, relative tolerance 1e-10,
diagonal relaxation 1e-10, and normality checks after iteration ten with
cutoff 1e-4. Its four-term structure is prespecified. Actual
coefficients are withheld: the diagnostic true\_vec is replaced by a
unit vector and use\_true=0. The returned normality value and iterates
are archived. These are the released iid-noise equations; under physical
OU error they are evaluated as a misspecified observation model, without
claiming a full adaptive OU-WENDy extension.

The MATLAB functions execute under GNU Octave 8.4.0 with statistics and
signal packages. Separate compatibility functions provide exact analytic
polynomial Jacobians in the declared feature order, exact values and
first derivatives of the specified bump with author normalization, and
the two-input pagemtimes operation used in covariance construction.
These replace Symbolic Toolbox and missing host operations. They do not
change radius selection, SVD, covariance updates, regression or stopping
logic. Bitwise agreement with a proprietary MATLAB runtime is not
asserted.

WmSINDy executes the author Python initializer, RK4 predictions,
weak-residual loss, gradient updates, adaptive window function, pruning
and least-squares refits. Every completed fit performs eight outer
cycles of 5000 Adam steps (40,000 total), with threshold 0.15, learning
rate 0.001, epsilon 1e-7, q=1 and decay 0.9. Noise is initialized to
zero. Legacy Adam is used with TensorFlow 2.20.0 / tf-keras 2.20.1.
Slots for new coefficient variables are created outside traced
functions, and the author loss functions are retraced after each window
update so changed variable-container shapes are actually used. Adam is
initialized independently for each recording; noise-variable slots and
iteration count continue through its eight cycles. The released
all-valid-convolution-row calculation is retained. Loss checkpoints,
eight coefficient iterates, window widths, spectral corners, optimizer
counts and the reconstructed noise signal are archived.

The compatibility control uses the author's Van der Pol example:
parameter 0.5, initial state (-2,1), duration ten, 1000 linspace points,
iid Gaussian amplitude 25 percent and seed one. Its nominal WmSINDy
prediction step 0.01 is retained although the linspace spacing is
10/999. The benchmark instead uses its exact grid spacing. Clean
trajectories are integrated with DOP853 at relative and absolute
tolerances 1e-12 for this control. The single control checks execution
and recovery and does not reproduce the published 50-run aggregate
figure. Reported comparison coefficient errors use the Frobenius norm.

The comparison contains 448 planned fits: four discovery algorithms and
three supplied-support parameter estimators, four observation cells and
16 records. Discovery success is exact support equality;
supplied-support estimators receive no structure-discovery score.
Failure counts remain in success denominators. Error medians and
bootstrap intervals condition on completed fits; all failures and their
logs are retained. Mesh differences are paired by recording. Wilson
limits accompany support counts in
\texttt{reports/full\_adaptive\_results.json}. Computational runtimes
are execution diagnostics, not cross-language speed claims. With one
oscillator and 16 paths per cell, these results do not justify universal
rankings or fine precision of support probabilities.

\subsection{S7. Information, window optimization and coefficient
replay}\label{s7.-information-window-optimization-and-coefficient-replay}

The configuration \texttt{configs/reviewer.json} specifies supplementary
calculations for Sections S9.7--S9.9. The Van der Pol calibration uses
the identical clean h=0.01 trajectory and coordinate standard deviations
as the complete-pipeline comparison. Noise variances are held fixed
under every parameter perturbation. Four independently free coefficients
are ordered as velocity in equation one, then position, velocity and
position-squared times velocity in equation two. The augmented DOP853
system integrates the two states, eight coefficient sensitivities and
four initial-state sensitivities, with relative tolerance 1e-11 and
absolute tolerance 1e-13. Stationary OU innovations give exact
sampled-trajectory information from these numerical sensitivities;
trapezoidal integration evaluates the continuous norm on grids from
h=0.04 down to 0.00125. Central coefficient perturbations of size 1e-5
check sensitivities at 101 points. The covariance matrices, joint
information, sensitivities and their derivatives are archived.

The scalar window optimum uses the complete cross-window residual
covariance and the normalization constraint in Section 4.6. Covariance
eigenvalues below 1e-10 or 1e-12 times the largest are discarded. The
normalization vector is projected onto the retained eigenspace, where
the constrained quadratic optimum is computed. The resulting influences
are checked for unit coefficient sensitivity and zero initial-state
sensitivity. Quadrature uses 4096 and 8192 intervals. The
spectral-cutoff dependence of the enriched 120-window family is retained
in S9.8 and numerical report; no global shape optimum is claimed. The
calculation uses generating parameters and noise covariance, so the
numbers concern an oracle local Gaussian reference.

The heterogeneous coefficient replay exactly reproduces all 3600
method/block/record-count errors of the 400-block aggregation
experiment. It saves coefficients in addition to norms and does not
supply new independent evidence. Bias and dispersion obey the empirical
squared-error identity in S5. The bias-fraction adjustment subtracts the
estimated sampling variance of the coefficient sample mean; neither
adjusted nor unadjusted fractions are claimed as exact population
quantities.

For the 16-path adaptive comparison, relative RMSE uses the complete
coefficient matrices, including falsely selected terms, and the same
Frobenius denominator as the error medians. Its 95\% intervals resample
independent records 5000 times. Comparing that RMSE with a local
unbiased information reference indicates the scale of remaining error;
it does not establish unbiasedness, regularity, calibrated uncertainty
or attained efficiency.

\subsection{S8. Efficiency protocols and complete
summaries}\label{s8.-efficiency-protocols-and-complete-summaries}

Theoretical proofs and their function-space assumptions are stated in
Sections 4.3--4.6 and in the standalone proof document. The
configuration \texttt{configs/efficiency.json} defines two experiments;
each executed notebook retains its own exact configuration and source
fingerprint. Numerical experiments run on private CPU Kaggle notebooks.
Synthetic OU paths use seeds 2100000--2100999 for the scalar experiment
and 2200000--2200127 for Van der Pol, independent of other studies.
Noise amplitudes, methods and window families are paired within seed.
Resampling uses 5000 independent-path bootstrap draws with generator
seed 2302600; paired comparisons resample one common path-index vector.
These are unadjusted exploratory intervals.

The scalar cutoff is the product of two quintic smoothstep ramps. For
distance \(d\) from an endpoint and width \(\delta\),
\(z=\min(1,\max(0,(d-\delta/4)/(3\delta/4)))\) and
\(\eta=z^3(10-15z+6z^2)\). Its derivative is included analytically in
the weak equation. The functions are compactly supported and \(C^2\),
which suffices for the implemented weak form; smooth approximation
yields the same covariance closure. Scalar experiments use 65536
quadrature intervals. The pilot and every final scalar estimate are
clipped to \([-2.5,0.5]\) for the bounded-risk summaries. Unbounded
error medians and every estimate are preserved. The ratio denominator
diagnostic uses the generating clean moment only after fitting; it
neither rejects observations nor changes estimates.

\subsubsection{S9.6. Complete adaptive author

-0.0204{]}. Under independent errors, WmSINDy recovered support on 10/16
paths, compared with 16/16 for fixed Hermite MSTLS. Its wide median
interval reflects selection failures despite the full training budget.
Thus the favorable OU result does not establish uniform superiority
across observation regimes. These unadjusted comparisons are descriptive
and limited to the frozen example.

The supplied-support comparison likewise concerns finite-sample
accuracy: adaptive WENDy improves the OU coefficient median relative to
these fixed-window fits, although its released noise model assumes
independent observations. This empirical behavior does not establish
calibrated uncertainty under OU error. Better window selection or
reconstruction can improve accuracy within the finite information
budget; it does not contradict the full-path infill bound.

All 448 planned fits are archived; 0 computational failures occurred.
Error medians condition on completed fits. Wilson intervals, coarse-grid
values, and paired mesh differences are retained in the reproducibility
report. With 16 paths, support fractions have substantial binomial
uncertainty. These conditions compare complete algorithms on one
oscillator and do not establish a general ranking or an asymptotic
plateau.

\subsubsection{S9.7. Van der Pol information
reference}\label{s9.7.-van-der-pol-information-reference}

The OU experiment in Section S9.6 has \(x'=a v\) and
\(v'=b x+c v+d x^2v\), with four independently free active coefficients
\((a,b,c,d)=(1,-1,1,-1)\). The initial state (2,0) is known. We hold the
calibrated coordinate noise variances fixed when differentiating the
mean, rather than re-estimating state-scaled variance at perturbed
parameters. The calibration is exactly that of the benchmark fine grid.
Coefficient and initial-state sensitivities solve
\(S'=JS+\partial_\theta f\) and \(Z'=JZ\), with \(S(0)=0\) and
\(Z(0)=I\). OU innovations give discrete information; the continuous
norm in Section 4.1 gives its limit.

For four active coefficients, the relative RMS reference is
\(\{\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal I_{\theta\theta}^{-1})\}^{1/2}/\|\theta\|_2\).
The weak initial-state-annihilation reference replaces information by
its Schur complement. Both are local covariance bounds, rather than
bounds on error medians or on every biased estimator.

\subsubsection{S9.9. Bias and dispersion of
aggregation}\label{s9.9.-bias-and-dispersion-of-aggregation}

The coefficient-preserving execution replays exactly the 400 independent
blocks in Table S5; it adds no independent samples. For normalized
coefficient errors, the empirical identity states that squared RMSE
equals squared bias plus squared dispersion. Table S11 reports the norm
of the sample mean error as bias and the square root of the second term
as dispersion. Bias here is an empirical estimate, not a proved
population limit. We also subtract sample-mean sampling variance when
estimating the squared-bias fraction; the adjustment is small for 400
blocks.
