\begin{abstract}
Boundary-free weak equations eliminate initial-state directions from
dynamical estimating equations. We show that this restriction is locally
efficient when initial states are nuisance parameters and quantify its
cost when they are known. For deterministic ODEs observed in stationary
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck error, the closed adjoint influence class has the
sharp covariance reference given by the inverse initial-state Schur
complement. An explicit generalized optimal test has endpoint jumps,
which compact tests approximate through boundary layers.
Correct-covariance GLS approaches this reference in a sequential
small-noise, dense-span limit. Discarding known initial-state
information raises the local RMSE reference by 29\% for Van der Pol and
almost doubles it for Lorenz. On 2000 shared Van der Pol records,
known-initial likelihood refinement has 23\% lower RMSE than weak GLS,
while OU weighting improves on iid weights by 4.2\%. Exact Hermite
covariance supports model weights for records with different initial
states. Independent instruments preserve corrected weak moment
identities at fixed noise and permit record-level sandwich inference
without fitting every initial state in the estimating equation.
Numerical tests assess span approximation, finite-noise accuracy and
interval calibration. The contribution is a statistical theory and
reproducible benchmark for weak estimation.

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Boundary-vanishing weak equations can lose information even when an
initial state is known, as recognized by orthogonality-condition
estimation {[}1{]} and recent weak identifiability work {[}2{]}. We
quantify this effect: the closed class of boundary-free weak influences
has exactly the local information available when the initial state is
treated as a nuisance parameter in the full path likelihood. For an
unknown initial state, this restriction causes no additional first-order
information loss; for a known initial state, the lost information is the
initial-sensitivity projection, and the retained information is its
Schur complement. The equivalence concerns local information.

The nuisance interpretation also connects single-record geometry to
repeated measurements with different initial conditions. Each new record
introduces its own initial state, while a common coefficient vector
remains the target. Corrected weak identities hold conditionally on
every initial state. Their local product-experiment information is the
sum of the record-specific Schur complements. At finite noise, valid
instruments must preserve those mean identities; merely inserting a
noisy design into an otherwise optimal weight recreates
errors-in-variables bias.

Sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics represents a vector field
through a library and selects active coefficients {[}3{]}. Integral
formulations reduce sensitivity to numerical differentiation {[}4{]},
and weak formulations transfer differentiation to test functions {[}5,
6{]}. Their regression matrices still contain noisy states. Statistical
consistency depends jointly on feature correction, residual covariance
and the acquisition limit.

The distinction between fixed-domain and increasing-domain observations
is classical in Gaussian process inference {[}7{]}. Ying established
fixed-domain results for exponential covariance parameters {[}8{]}, and
Zhang used equivalence of Gaussian measures to explain inconsistent
covariance estimation in the Matérn family {[}9{]}. Those results
concern covariance parameters. Here the covariance is common across
parameters, and the parameter enters the deterministic mean through an
ODE. The same measure-equivalence mechanism supplies an
estimator-independent limit, with an explicit norm determined by
dynamical sensitivities.

Continuous-time regression with correlated errors has an established
optimal-design theory, including optimal signed measures with endpoint
masses. Dette, Pepelyshev and Zhigljavsky derive continuous-record best
linear unbiased estimators and efficient discrete designs for triangular
covariance kernels {[}10{]}. Dynamical sensitivities define a local
regression in this framework. Finite limiting precision and endpoint
information are therefore precedents for our weak-estimation analysis.

The continuum-data theory of WSINDy studies uncorrelated measurement
errors and library-dependent consistency {[}11{]}. Fixed physical
correlation time defines a different experiment: adjacent correlations
approach one as the mesh is refined. Correlation induced by filtering
independently sampled errors also requires distinguishing a physical
filter scale from a fixed number of sample indices. Our analysis
concerns a fixed covariance process observed at increasingly dense
times.

The contribution is a theory and benchmark for boundary-free estimation
with nuisance initial states. Theorems 1 and 2 give the fixed-horizon
obstruction and the exact adjoint class with its sharp information
reference. The OU construction supplies an explicit optimal generalized
test; Corollary 1 explains sequential dense-span GLS efficiency.
Proposition 3 supplies full polynomial residual covariance for computing
model weights. Theorem 3 extends the conditional weak identity to
heterogeneous records, using independent instruments to obtain
fixed-noise consistency and a record-level sandwich. Its local
information reference is the sum of the individual nuisance-adjusted
informations. Established Gaussian projection, BLUE, GMM and
data-fission theory provide the statistical tools {[}10, 12--14{]}; the
precise adjoint restriction and its quantitative dynamical cost form the
central result.

\section{Related estimation
methods}\label{related-estimation-methods}

Statistical ODE estimation has long used smoothing to reduce repeated
forward solves: spline least squares {[}15{]}, nonparametric gradient
matching {[}16{]}, measurement-error regression {[}17{]} and root-\(n\)
smooth-and-match estimation {[}18{]}. Direct integral estimators have
root-\(n\) consistency for suitable linear-in-function parameterizations
{[}19{]}. Orthogonality-condition estimation already supplies weak
moments, GMM weighting and boundary-information adaptations {[}1{]}.
Thus neither the qualitative boundary-loss idea nor optimally weighted
moments originates here.

Generalized profiling can be asymptotically efficient without explicitly
solving the ODE, under its approximation and i.i.d. sampling assumptions
{[}20, 21{]}. One-step likelihood refinement of an ODE pilot is also
established {[}22{]}; Dattner and Klaassen {[}19{]} discuss such
refinements as a direction beyond their direct-estimator result. These
methods provide efficient full-observation references. The question is
the optimum available to boundary-free weak equations, which also
provide tractable moments for library-based structural discovery. The
present theorem assumes supplied identifiable structure; the one-step
computation below is a likelihood reference on the same correlated-noise
records. These small supplied-structure examples remain tractable for
likelihood refinement; the comparison concerns information and accuracy.

Gaussian regression geometry and small-noise efficiency belong to
classical statistical theory {[}23--25{]}. Here those principles are
connected to the dynamical adjoint range. The distinction from prior
qualitative boundary observations is the exact covariance-dual class,
its sharp Schur-complement reference and the explicit OU representative.

WENDy treats weak parameter estimation as an errors-in-variables problem
and iteratively updates a linearized residual covariance {[}26{]}. It
retains perturbations of both the feature matrix and the response. We
use the same coupled residual and compute every Gaussian order for
polynomial features under the specified OU model. First-order truncation
recovers the structure motivating weak reweighting, with temporal
covariance inserted explicitly. Residual covariance is distinct from the
covariance of the nonlinear coefficient estimator.

WENDy-MLE optimizes an approximate Gaussian weak-residual likelihood
with parameter-dependent covariance and analytic derivatives {[}27{]}.
Its likelihood contains both a log determinant and a covariance-weighted
residual term; its finite-noise objective differs from frozen-weight
GLS. Tran and Bortz select local test radii by estimating quadrature
error {[}28{]}, while Fourier Weak SINDy selects informative sinusoidal
frequencies spectrally {[}29{]}. These are relevant precedents for weak
likelihoods and test design. The complementary question here is the
complete adjoint influence class, its sharp initial-state information
loss and the explicit OU optimal representative. Our contribution
quantifies the attainable information of the complete weak class.

Finite-noise bias and uncertainty are also studied directly for
WENDy-IRLS {[}30{]}. Our bias/dispersion reporting concerns the
specified correlated-noise experiment and complements these broader
coverage studies.

Errors-in-variables effects have a substantial statistical literature
{[}31{]}. Gaussian correction of nonlinear functions of a noisy mean
also precedes weak identification {[}32{]}. Hermite correction removes
pointwise polynomial bias when marginal noise variance is known; random
design--response coupling remains in coefficient estimates. Generalized
smoothing and profiling estimate ODE parameters through a different
compromise between model fidelity and observation error {[}20{]}. The
information bound applies to these alternatives when they observe the
same path experiment.

WmSINDy combines weak regression with simultaneous dynamics and noise
reconstruction {[}33{]}. We distinguish the large fixed-window
regression-component study from a separate paired execution of the
complete adaptive author WSINDy, WENDy and WmSINDy pipelines. The latter
retains noise estimation or reconstruction and adaptive test-function
selection; WENDy supplies structure for parameter estimation.
Ensemble-SINDy uses bootstrap aggregation to quantify feature inclusion
{[}34{]}. Resampling one recorded path and acquiring independent
physical recordings are different operations: the latter changes the
number of independent path experiments in the information calculation.

\subsection{Complementarity with orthogonality-condition
estimation}\label{complementarity-with-orthogonality-condition-estimation}

Brunel et al. {[}1{]} use a nonparametric trajectory proxy,
orthogonality conditions and GMM in an independent-observation,
increasing-point experiment, with a formulation extending to delay
equations. They already recognize boundary-information loss and describe
adaptations using known boundary values. Our fully observed
deterministic polynomial setting is narrower in model structure. It
instead describes physical OU noise on a fixed horizon, the exact closed
adjoint influence class, its sharp Schur-complement reference and an
explicit optimal OU test. The small-noise dense-span result and the
fixed-noise independent-record result below concern different asymptotic
experiments from their increasing-point analysis. These results
complement each other; neither a first use of weak GMM nor a strict
dominance claim is intended.

\section{Observation model and weak
equations}\label{observation-model-and-weak-equations}

Let \(x_\theta:[0,T]\to\mathbb R^d\) be a deterministic ODE trajectory.
In the polynomial setting,

\[
x'_i=f_i(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\xi^*_{ji}\vartheta_j(x),\qquad i=1,\ldots,d.
\]

The library contains all true terms. Observations are
\(Y(t)=x_\theta(t)+\eta_C(t)\), with independent stationary OU
coordinates {[}35{]} and common-across-parameter covariance

\[
C_j(s,t)=\sigma_j^2\exp(-|s-t|/\tau_j),\qquad \sigma_j>0,\quad\tau_j>0.
\]

The continuous record is a statistical reference experiment; actual data
are evaluations \(y_n=Y(t_n)\) at mesh \(h\). The initial state
\(u=x(0)\) is known in the baseline acquisition experiment; Sections 5
and 7 compare known and unknown \(u\) explicitly. The symbol \(Z\)
denotes its fundamental sensitivity matrix, \(h\) the observation mesh,
\(\epsilon\) a scalar noise amplitude, and \(C\) the continuous
covariance operator. Finite weak covariance matrices are denoted by
\(\Sigma\). In Corollary 1, \(C\) is the unit-amplitude covariance shape
and the physical covariance is \(\epsilon^2 C\).

Fix \(K\) test functions \(\phi_k\in C_c^2(0,T)\). Their positions and
full support widths \(L\) remain fixed as \(h\) decreases. Let
\(\mathcal Q_h\) be composite trapezoidal quadrature with weights
\(w_n\). Clean weak quantities are

\[
G^0_{kj}=\int_0^T\phi_k\vartheta_j(x)\,dt,\qquad
b^0_{ki}=-\int_0^T\phi'_kx_i\,dt,\qquad b^0=G^0\xi^*.
\]

Noisy matrices use corrected polynomial features
\(\widetilde\vartheta_j\), defined in Section 6:

\[
(G_h)_{kj}=\mathcal Q_h(\phi_k\widetilde\vartheta_j(y)),\qquad
(b_h)_{ki}=-\mathcal Q_h(\phi'_ky_i).
\]

Physical OU infill sets \(\rho(h)=\exp(-h/\tau)\) with fixed \(T,\tau\).
An iid discrete control has independent error of fixed per-sample
variance. Fixed-index AR(1) instead keeps \(\rho<1\) constant, making
the physical correlation time \(-h/\log\rho\to0\). These models have
different continuum limits.

\section{Fixed-horizon information
limits}\label{fixed-horizon-information-limits}

\subsection{OU translation
geometry}\label{ou-translation-geometry}

The stationary OU process satisfies
\(d\eta_{C,j}=-\eta_{C,j}dt/\tau_j+\sqrt{2\sigma_j^2/\tau_j}\,dW_j\),
with an independent initial value \(\eta_{C,j}(0)\sim N(0,\sigma_j^2)\).
Its Cameron--Martin space is \(H^1([0,T];\mathbb R^d)\) as a set, with
norm

\[
\|g\|_{\mathcal H}^2=\sum_j\left\{\frac{g_j(0)^2}{\sigma_j^2}+
\frac{\tau_j}{2\sigma_j^2}\int_0^T(g'_j+g_j/\tau_j)^2\,dt\right\}.
\]

Transforming a path into its initial value and innovation path
\(z(t)-z(0)+\tau_j^{-1}\int_0^tz(s)ds\) separates the normal initial
observation from scaled Brownian motion. Translation by \(g\) gives an
initial shift and Brownian drift \(g'+g/\tau_j\). The classical
translation theorem {[}36{]} gives the norm. Equivalently,

\[
\|g\|_{\mathcal H}^2=\sum_j\left\{\frac{\tau_j}{2\sigma_j^2}\int_0^T(g'_j)^2dt+
\frac1{2\sigma_j^2\tau_j}\int_0^Tg_j^2dt+
\frac{g_j(0)^2+g_j(T)^2}{2\sigma_j^2}\right\}.
\]

The endpoint terms encode stationary initial noise. Zero initial error
would define a different experiment.

\textbf{Theorem 1 (fixed-horizon two-point bound; proof in S1.1).}
Suppose the mean trajectories belong to \(\mathcal H\) and share the
stated positive OU covariance. For \(\delta=\|\theta-\eta\|>0\) and
\(d=\|x_\theta-x_\eta\|_{\mathcal H}\), every finite-grid estimator
satisfies

\[
\max_{v\in\{\theta,\eta\}}P_v\{\|\widehat\theta_h-v\|\geq\delta/2\}
\geq\Phi(-d/2)>0.
\]

Its maximal two-point squared risk is at least
\((\delta^2/4)\Phi(-d/2)\). Thus dense-grid consistency throughout a
nontrivial fixed-horizon family is excluded by Gaussian path equivalence
{[}9, 37{]}.

\subsection{Continuous-record information and scalar weak
ratios}\label{continuous-record-information-and-scalar-weak-ratios}

\textbf{Proposition 1 (continuous-record information).} If
\(\theta\mapsto x_\theta\) is differentiable in \(\mathcal H\) and
\(s_r=\partial_{\theta_r}x_\theta\), continuous-record Fisher
information is

\[
\mathcal I_{rs}(\theta)=\langle s_r,s_s\rangle_{\mathcal H}.
\]

The Gaussian mean-score identity proves the assertion: the directional
score has variance equal to the squared Cameron-Martin norm of its
sensitivity. Its inverse is the Cramér--Rao covariance bound for regular
unbiased estimators, when nonsingular. Biased estimators require the
corresponding bias derivative; finite-horizon maximum likelihood need
not attain the unbiased bound.

For \(x_a(t)=x_0e^{at}\) with known \(x_0\), \(s(t)=x_0te^{at}\) and

\[
\mathcal I_T(a)=\frac{\tau x_0^2}{2\sigma^2}
\int_0^Te^{2at}[1+(a+\tau^{-1})t]^2\,dt.
\]

On a uniform grid, exact innovations give

\[
\mathcal I_h(a)=\frac{s(0)^2}{\sigma^2}+
\frac{\sum_{n=1}^N[s(t_n)-\rho s(t_{n-1})]^2}{\sigma^2(1-\rho^2)}
\longrightarrow\mathcal I_T(a),\qquad\rho=e^{-h/\tau}.
\]

Nested grids give increasing information bounded by \(\mathcal I_T\).
The limit follows from a Riemann sum of squared sensitivity innovations.
Statistical saturation therefore concerns the full experiment.

For \(D_\phi=\int\phi x_a\ne0\), a scalar weak ratio has limit

\[
\widehat a_h=\frac{-\mathcal Q_h(\phi'Y)}{\mathcal Q_h(\phi Y)}
\longrightarrow a+\frac{A}{D_\phi+B},\qquad
A=\int(-\phi'-a\phi)\eta_C,\quad B=\int\phi\eta_C.
\]

OU path continuity gives almost sure convergence of the quadratures. The
weights \(\phi\) and \(-\phi'-a\phi\) are independent: dependence would
make a nonzero compactly supported function solve a homogeneous
first-order equation. The OU kernel is strictly positive on nonzero
weights, so \((A,B)\) is nondegenerate Gaussian. Positive conditional
variance of \(A\) given \(B\) and positive denominator density at zero
imply infinite absolute and squared error moments of the unguarded
ratio. Its empirical summaries should use quantiles or explicit guards.

The influence \(\ell=(-\phi'-a\phi)/D_\phi\) has Gaussian variance

\[
V_\phi=\iint\ell(u)C(u,v)\ell(v)dudv.
\]

Differentiating the weak identity gives \(\int\ell s=1\).
Cauchy--Schwarz in the covariance RKHS gives

\[
1\leq V_\phi\mathcal I_T(a),\qquad
\operatorname{Eff}_\phi=[\mathcal I_T(a)V_\phi]^{-1}\leq1.
\]

This efficiency concerns the Gaussian linearization.

\section{Boundary-free weak class and
efficiency}\label{boundary-free-weak-class-and-efficiency}

\subsection{Exact boundary-free influence
class}\label{exact-boundary-free-influence-class}

Along \(x_{\theta,u}\), put \(J(t)=D_xf(x_{\theta,u}(t),\theta)\) and
\(Z(t)=\partial_u x_{\theta,u}(t)\). Assume
\(J\in C^1([0,T];\mathbb R^{d\times d})\), and let the invertible
fundamental matrix satisfy \(Z'=JZ\), \(Z(0)=I_d\). Put
\(\mathcal D v=v'-Jv\) and \(\mathcal D^*\phi=-\phi'-J^T\phi\). Sensor
coordinates have independent stationary OU covariance with fixed
positive scales. Its Cameron--Martin space \(\mathcal H\) is \(H^1\)
with the OU inner product. Write \(\mathcal E=\mathcal H'\) for its
continuous dual, equipped with the covariance norm

\[
\|\lambda\|_C^2=\langle\lambda,C\lambda\rangle,
\qquad
\langle C^{-1}g,v\rangle=\langle g,v\rangle_{\mathcal H}.
\]

This definition includes ordinary integrated influences and endpoint
evaluation measures. General dual influences act through the \(L^2(P)\)
closure of integrated Gaussian observations, through Gaussian
observations. Their variance norm is \(\|\lambda\|_C\); the noise path
itself need not belong to \(\mathcal H\).

\textbf{Proposition 2 (exact range and closure).} In \(L^2\),

\[
\mathcal D^*H_0^1
=\left\{\ell\in L^2:\int_0^T Z(t)^T\ell(t)dt=0\right\}.
\]

The range of compactly supported smooth tests is dense in this space in
\(L^2\). Its closure in \(\mathcal E\) is exactly

\[
\mathcal M=\overline{\mathcal D^*C_c^\infty}^{\|\cdot\|_C}
=\{\lambda\in\mathcal E:\langle\lambda,Z_j\rangle=0, j=1,\ldots,d\}.
\]

Consequently \(\mathcal M\) has codimension \(d\).

\textbf{Proof.} Since \(Z^T\mathcal D^*\phi=-(Z^T\phi)'\), zero endpoint
values imply the moment constraint. Conversely, for an \(L^2\) influence
with zero moment,

\[
\phi(t)=-Z(t)^{-T}\int_0^t Z(s)^T\ell(s)ds
\]

belongs to \(H_0^1\) and satisfies \(\mathcal D^*\phi=\ell\).
Approximation in \(H_0^1\) by \(C_c^\infty\) and continuity of
\(\mathcal D^*:H_0^1\to L^2\) give \(L^2\) density. For the stronger
closure statement, a function \(v\in\mathcal H\) annihilates every
\(\mathcal D^*\phi\) if and only if \(\mathcal D v=0\) distributionally,
hence \(v=Zc\). The Hahn--Banach annihilator characterization of a
closed subspace then gives the stated closure in \(\mathcal H'\). The
columns of \(Z\) are linearly independent elements of \(\mathcal H\), so
their dual constraints have rank \(d\). \(\square\)

The exact range uses zero-endpoint Sobolev tests; literal compact tests
require closure. The covariance norm is equivalent to the dual Sobolev
norm for this positive OU model.

\textbf{Lemma 1 (a concrete dense test family).} Let \(V_m\) be
vector-valued continuous piecewise-linear splines on the nested uniform
dyadic partitions of \([0,T]\), with zero values at both endpoints. Then

\[
\overline{\bigcup_m\mathcal D^*V_m}^{\|\cdot\|_C}=\mathcal M.
\]

\textbf{Proof.} Given \(v\in H_0^1\), choose \(\psi\in C_c^\infty\)
close to \(v\) in \(H^1\). Its nodal interpolant \(I_m\psi\in V_m\)
satisfies \(\|I_m\psi-\psi\|_{H^1}\leq c\Delta_m\|\psi''\|_{L^2}\),
where \(\Delta_m\) is the mesh width. The union of \(V_m\) is therefore
dense in \(H_0^1\). Continuity of \(\mathcal D^*:H_0^1\to L^2\) and of
the embedding \(L^2\hookrightarrow\mathcal H'\) transfers this
approximation to the covariance norm. Proposition 2 identifies the
closure of \(\mathcal D^*H_0^1\) with \(\mathcal M\). \(\square\)

These splines are admissible zero-endpoint Sobolev tests; they need not
vanish in an endpoint neighborhood. If literal smooth compact tests are
required, approximate each spline in \(H_0^1\) by such tests. Thus Lemma
1 provides an explicit family for the density condition in Corollary 1.
The experiments use finite multiscale approximations.

\subsection{Sharp information and optimal
influence}\label{sharp-information-and-optimal-influence}

Let \(S=[S_1,\ldots,S_p]\in\mathcal H^p\) be coefficient sensitivities,
and form the joint information blocks for \(S\) and \(Z\). Assume
\(\mathcal I_{uu}\) and the Schur complement are positive definite. Set

\[
R=S-Z\mathcal I_{uu}^{-1}\mathcal I_{u\theta},
\qquad
\mathcal I_e=\mathcal I_{\theta\theta}
-\mathcal I_{\theta u}\mathcal I_{uu}^{-1}\mathcal I_{u\theta}.
\]

Projection and best linear unbiased estimation in correlated continuous
regression are classical {[}10{]}. Here the adjoint-range
characterization identifies exactly which dynamical weak influences obey
that projection.

\textbf{Theorem 2 (sharp boundary-free influence covariance).} Let
\(\Lambda=[\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_p]\in\mathcal M^p\) satisfy
\(\langle\lambda_r,S_s\rangle=\delta_{rs}\). Its Gaussian influence
covariance obeys

\[
V(\Lambda)\succeq\mathcal I_e^{-1}.
\]

The bound is attained in \(\mathcal M\) by the matrix-valued dual
influence

\[
\Lambda_*=C^{-1}R\,\mathcal I_e^{-1}.
\]

It is the infimum for normalized compact-test influences. It is a
minimum among literal compact tests only when \(\Lambda_*\) belongs to
their adjoint range.

\textbf{Proof.} The \(\mathcal H\) projection makes
\(\langle C^{-1}R,Z\rangle=0\) and
\(\langle C^{-1}R,S\rangle=\mathcal I_e\). Thus \(\Lambda_*\) meets both
constraints and has covariance \(\mathcal I_e^{-1}\). The difference
\(\Delta=\Lambda-\Lambda_*\) pairs to zero with both \(S\) and \(Z\),
hence with \(R\). Covariance cross terms vanish and

\[
V(\Lambda)=\mathcal I_e^{-1}+V(\Delta).
\]

For attainability as an infimum, approximate each column of
\(\Lambda_*\) by compact-test influences in covariance norm. Their
normalization matrices converge to \(I_p\) by continuous pairing with
\(S\); right-multiplication by their inverse transpose enforces exact
normalization while preserving the range and covariance convergence.
\(\square\)

For an unknown initial state, \(\mathcal I_e\) is the full likelihood
nuisance-adjusted information, so this weak restriction has no further
first-order cost. When the initial state is known, the information loss
is exactly
\(\mathcal I_{\theta u}\mathcal I_{uu}^{-1}\mathcal I_{u\theta}\);
covariance inflation is
\(\mathcal I_e^{-1}-\mathcal I_{\theta\theta}^{-1}\). There is no
direction-independent scalar loss percentage in general. When \(u\) is
known, \(Y(0)-u\) also reveals the initial stationary measurement error,
which remains correlated with later observations. The unrestricted score
can exploit that observation while the weak influence must retain its
initial-sensitivity annihilation constraint.

\subsection{Explicit optimal OU
test}\label{explicit-optimal-ou-test}

For a scalar coordinate with \(g\in H^2\), integration by parts in the
stationary OU norm gives the dual measure

\[
C^{-1}g=
\frac{g-\tau^2g''}{2\sigma^2\tau}\,dt
+\frac{g(0)-\tau g'(0)}{2\sigma^2}\delta_0
+\frac{g(T)+\tau g'(T)}{2\sigma^2}\delta_T.
\]

For independent coordinates the formula applies coordinatewise, using
their respective scales. If only \(g\in H^1\), the Riesz representative
remains well-defined in \(\mathcal H'\) but need not be a density plus
finite endpoint measures.

For a smooth optimal representative write
\(\Lambda_*=m(t)dt+m_0\delta_0+m_T\delta_T\). On the open interval an
adjoint test matrix is

\[
\Phi_*(t)=-Z(t)^{-T}
\left[Z(0)^Tm_0+\int_0^t Z(s)^Tm(s)ds\right].
\]

Extend it by zero outside the observation interval. Its endpoint jumps
supply \(m_0=-\Phi_*(0+)\) and \(m_T=\Phi_*(T-)\), and distributionally
\(\mathcal D^*\Phi_*=\Lambda_*\). These formulas include the initial
atom; omitting it in the cumulative integral is incorrect. The
annihilation condition supplies the terminal jump. Cutoff and smoothing
approximate this generalized test in covariance norm. These measures
describe a statistical reference; the implemented estimators keep
compact tests and add no explicit boundary observation terms.

For scalar exponential dynamics, \(z=e^{at}\), \(s=x_0te^{at}\),
\(k=\mathcal I_{au}/\mathcal I_{uu}\) and \(g=e^{at}(x_0t-k)\). Define

\[
A_0=\frac{(1-\tau^2a^2)x_0}{2\sigma^2\tau},\qquad
B_0=\frac{-(1-\tau^2a^2)k-2\tau^2ax_0}{2\sigma^2\tau},
\]

\[
q_0=\frac{-k-\tau(x_0-ak)}{2\sigma^2},\qquad
F_j(t)=\int_0^t s^je^{2as}ds.
\]

Then

\[
\phi_*(t)=-\frac{e^{-at}}{\mathcal I_e}
[q_0+A_0F_1(t)+B_0F_0(t)].
\]

For \(a\ne0\), \(F_0=\operatorname{expm1}(2at)/(2a)\) and
\(F_1=[e^{2at}(2at-1)+1]/(4a^2)\); their continuous limits at \(a=0\)
are \(t\) and \(t^2/2\). A pilot coefficient substitutes into this
expression before a compact cutoff is applied. The explicit multivariate
fundamental-matrix expression is an analytical identity whose numerical
inversion depends on conditioning; strongly contracting dynamics can
make that inversion ill-conditioned.

\subsection{Correct-covariance GLS and dense
spans}\label{correct-covariance-gls-and-dense-spans}

For tests \(\phi_k\), put \(\lambda_k=\mathcal D^*\phi_k\),
\(D_{kr}=\langle\lambda_k,S_r\rangle\) and
\(\Sigma_{k\ell}=\langle\lambda_k,C\lambda_\ell\rangle\). Remove exact
linear dependencies. If \(D\) has full column rank, covariance-weighted
weak regression has local influence and covariance

\[
P=(D^T\Sigma^{-1}D)^{-1}D^T\Sigma^{-1},\qquad
V_K=(D^T\Sigma^{-1}D)^{-1}.
\]

WENDy's covariance iteration motivates this construction {[}26{]}, and
physical-OU covariance changes its temporal weights. Under polynomial
features Proposition 3 supplies the complete coupled residual weights.
Its order-one term determines the small-noise efficient influence;
higher orders correct residual uncertainty at finite amplitude without
by themselves removing coefficient-estimator bias.

The same leading map arises from a parameter-dependent Gaussian weak
likelihood of the form studied by WENDy-MLE {[}27{]}, provided the
covariance shape is correct and the local solution is regular. For fixed
dimension write \(r(\theta)=\epsilon r_1+o_p(\epsilon)\),
\(\partial_\theta r=-D+o_p(1)\) and
\(\Sigma_\epsilon(\theta)=\epsilon^2\Sigma_0(\theta)\), with smooth
positive definite \(\Sigma_0\). The objective
\(\log\det\Sigma_\epsilon+r^T\Sigma_\epsilon^{-1}r\) has
covariance-derivative and log-determinant score terms of order
\(O_p(1)\) in an \(O_p(\epsilon)\) neighborhood; its mean-residual score
is of order \(O_p(\epsilon^{-1})\). The local score equation therefore
gives

\[
\widehat\theta-\theta=\epsilon
(D^T\Sigma_0^{-1}D)^{-1}D^T\Sigma_0^{-1}r_1+o_p(\epsilon).
\]

Thus the log determinant and covariance derivatives distinguish
finite-noise weak likelihood from frozen GLS, while both share the
correct-covariance leading influence under these assumptions. This
connects Corollary 1 to a regular local weak-likelihood estimator; its
assumptions concern a regular local optimizer and consistent covariance
calibration.

\textbf{Corollary 1 (small-noise, dense-span efficiency).} Let the noise
be \(\epsilon\eta_C\) with known covariance shape \(C\). Assume a smooth
identifiable parameterization, a generating parameter interior to a
compact parameter set, consistent pilots, consistent covariance-shape
weights and quadrature error \(o(\epsilon)\). For each fixed finite test
family assume a regular local weak solution with the GLS linearization.
For the covariance conclusion additionally assume uniform integrability
of \(\|\epsilon^{-1}(\widehat\theta_{K,\epsilon}-\theta)\|^2\). A
sufficient implementation uses a uniformly stable smooth local solve,
Gaussian moment control on the local Taylor remainder, and bounded
fallback outside the regular region on a bad event whose probability is
\(o(\epsilon^2)\). Then

\[
\epsilon^{-1}(\widehat\theta_{K,\epsilon}-\theta)
\Rightarrow N(0,V_K),\qquad
\epsilon^{-2}\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat\theta_{K,\epsilon})\to V_K.
\]

If nested test-influence spans are dense in \(\mathcal M\), then

\[
V_K\longrightarrow\mathcal I_{e,C}^{-1}.
\]

\textbf{Proof.} For fixed \(K\), a first-order expansion of the weak
equations gives \(D(\widehat\theta-\theta)=\epsilon r_1+o_p(\epsilon)\)
with the GLS map above. Consistent pilots and weights change the leading
map by \(o_p(1)\); derivatives of pilot-dependent tests multiply the
zero clean residual. Gaussianity gives the distributional assertion; the
stated risk condition justifies covariance convergence. In the Gaussian
dual space, finite-family information is the Gram matrix of the
orthogonal projection of \(C^{-1}R\) onto its test span. Density gives
projection convergence, and nested spans give increasing information;
continuity of inversion at positive definite \(\mathcal I_e\) gives
covariance convergence. \(\square\)

The conclusion is sequential: first small noise and controlled
quadrature for fixed \(K\), then dense spans. A diagonal joint limit
requires uniform remainder, conditioning and covariance-weight control
requiring a separate rate analysis. This theorem concerns regular
WENDy-like weak estimation with correct covariance and supplied
identifiable structure. The conditional result covers the leading GLS
map, with supplied identifiable structure. A fixed basis cutoff must be
compatible with the density assumption; discarding an informative
direction permanently can invalidate it.

\section{Exact polynomial weak-residual
covariance}\label{exact-polynomial-weak-residual-covariance}

\subsection{Finite Hermite
expansion}\label{finite-hermite-expansion}

Assume a common \(\tau\) for independent coordinates, and set
\(v_j=\sigma_j^2\). Define \(H_0=1\), \(H_1=u\),
\(H_{q+1}=uH_q-qvH_{q-1}\) and corrected monomials
\(\widetilde\vartheta_\beta(y)=\prod_jH_{\beta_j}(y_j;v_j)\). Their
expectation given \(x\) is \(x^\beta\). This Gaussian debiasing
construction {[}32{]} centers the polynomial residual at the true
coefficients.

For continuum residual \(r_{ki}=b_{ki}-(G\xi^*)_{ki}\), polynomial
expansion gives

\[
r_{ki}=\sum_{1\leq|\alpha|\leq p_*}\int_0^Tc_{ki,\alpha}(t)H_\alpha(\eta_C(t);v)dt,
\]

where \(p_*\geq1\) is the relevant maximum degree,
\(H_\alpha=\prod_jH_{\alpha_j}\) and

\[
c_{ki,\alpha}=-\mathbf1_{\{\alpha=e_i\}}\phi'_k
-\phi_k\frac{\partial^\alpha f_i(x)}{\alpha!}.
\]

The response and feature perturbations share this coefficient and retain
their cross-covariance.

\textbf{Proposition 3 (exact covariance).} Under these assumptions,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(r_{ki},r_{\ell m})=
\sum_{1\leq|\alpha|\leq p_*}\alpha!\prod_jv_j^{\alpha_j}
\int_0^T\int_0^Tc_{ki,\alpha}(s)c_{\ell m,\alpha}(t)
e^{-|\alpha||s-t|/\tau}\,ds\,dt.
\]

\textbf{Proof.} The generating function \(\exp(zu-vz^2/2)\) yields
\(E[H_q(U;v)H_r(V;v)]=\mathbf1_{q=r}q!v^q\rho^q\) for jointly Gaussian
variables of variance \(v\) and correlation \(\rho\). This is the
Hermite form of classical Gaussian moment identities {[}38{]}.
Coordinate independence gives orthogonality between different
multi-indices and factor \(\alpha!\prod_jv_j^{\alpha_j}\). Substitute
\(\rho=e^{-|s-t|/\tau}\) and integrate the finite expansion. \(\square\)

The order-one covariance supplies the GLS weights required by Corollary
1. Higher orders refine residual weights at finite amplitude;
coefficient bias also depends on random-design terms. Order \(q\) has
correlation time \(\tau/q\). Higher orders need not be negligible at
moderate amplitude. Different windows and equations remain correlated
despite independent sensor coordinates. The covariance is exact for a
supplied clean trajectory and true polynomial; plugging in estimates
introduces another approximation.

\subsection{Raw features and efficient
evaluation}\label{raw-features-and-efficient-evaluation}

For correction variance \(\widehat v\), define

\[
\bar f=\exp\left[\frac12\sum_j(v_j-\widehat v_j)\partial_j^2\right]f.
\]

The exponential terminates for polynomials. Using \(\bar f\) in \(c\)
gives covariance, while mean residual is
\(\int\phi_k[f_i(x)-\bar f_i(x)]dt\). Raw monomials use
\(\widehat v=0\). Mean bias and covariance are separate outputs.

On a uniform grid, collect weighted coefficients into an \(N\times Kd\)
matrix \(U_\alpha\). Each term is
\(\alpha!v^\alpha U_\alpha^TR_{q,h}U_\alpha\), where
\((R_{q,h})_{nm}=\rho_q^{|n-m|}\) and \(\rho_q=e^{-qh/\tau}\). For
vector \(u\),

\[
a_n=u_n+\rho_qa_{n-1},\qquad z_n=u_n+\rho_qz_{n+1},\qquad
R_{q,h}u=a+z-u,
\]

with zero exterior initialization. Each multi-index costs \(O(N(Kd)^2)\)
time and \(O(NKd+(Kd)^2)\) memory, avoiding an \(N\times N\) temporal
matrix. An independent fixed-variance nugget has additional
diagonal-in-time covariance; its linear weak contribution is \(O(h)\).
The exact mixed-noise formula is in the supplement.

\subsection{Temporal
misspecification}\label{temporal-misspecification}

For a fixed moment family with correct marginal correction, the
independent-record variance under weights \(W\) is
\(P_W\Omega_{\mathrm{true}}P_W^\top/n\). The same sandwich describes the
leading small-noise single-record variance using the order-one residual
covariance. Incorrect temporal weights can reduce efficiency while
preserving the corrected mean identity; incorrect correction variances
can invalidate that identity. In S12, assuming \(\tau/2\) or \(2\tau\)
changes only weighting, whereas fitting variances changes correction as
well. Replication allows consistent estimation of both OU range and
variances under the shared-trajectory construction.

\section{Independent records with nuisance initial
states}\label{independent-records-with-nuisance-initial-states}

\subsection{Conditional moment identities and independent
instruments}\label{conditional-moment-identities-and-independent-instruments}

Let independent records have means \(x_{\theta,u_r}\), with a common
coefficient vector and a separate unknown initial state for each record.
Hermite-corrected weak moments obey
\(E\{m_r(\theta_0,\nu_0)\mid u_r\}=0\). Consequently the initial-state
distribution can remain unspecified. At finite noise, a same-record
pilot used in \(D_r^\top\Omega_r^{-1}\) can correlate with the residual
and spoil that identity. We use an explicit independent-instrument
construction to state a feasible fixed-noise result.

For known OU covariance \(C\), draw independent \(\zeta_r\sim N(0,C)\)
and set \(Y_r^\pm=Y_r\pm\zeta_r\). These independent Gaussian views have
covariance \(2C\) {[}14{]}. Instruments \(L_r^\pm\) use their own view
and independent training data; moments use the opposite view with its
doubled correction variance. Define \(B_r=(L_r^+G_r^-+L_r^-G_r^+)/2\)
and \(a_r=(L_r^+b_r^-+L_r^-b_r^+)/2\).

\textbf{Assumptions (A1)--(A5).} Trajectories and sensitivities are
regular on a compact initial-state set; records and randomizations are
independent with known positive covariance; the instruments obey the
stated view separation and uniform moment bounds; the average derivative
has a nonsingular limit and the centered record scores satisfy a
Lindeberg CLT; the average instrumented quadrature defect is
\(o(n^{-1/2})\). The precise numbered conditions are in S16.1.

\textbf{Theorem 3 (heterogeneous-record weak estimating equations).}
Under (A1)--(A5), conditional on the independent training data,

\[
\widehat\theta_n=\left(\sum_r B_r\right)^{-1}\sum_r a_r
\xrightarrow{P}\theta_0,\qquad
\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_0)
\Rightarrow N(0,B^{-1}V_\psi B^{-\top}),
\]

where \(B=\lim n^{-1}\sum E B_r\) and
\(V_\psi=\lim n^{-1}\sum\operatorname{Var}(a_r-B_r\theta_0)\). The
empirical covariance of the coefficient-dimensional record scores
consistently estimates its sandwich. The proof uses
\(E(L_r^+m_r^-\mid u_r)=0\) and the symmetric identity, followed by the
standard estimating-equation CLT {[}12, 13{]}; see S16.1.

\subsection{Model weights and the product information
reference}\label{model-weights-and-the-product-information-reference}

For deterministic clean-trajectory instruments, \(D_r=E G_r\) and
Proposition 3's full covariance \(\Omega_r\) yield
\(L_r=D_r^\top\Omega_r^{-1}\). The oracle fixed-family covariance is
\((\sum_rD_r^\top\Omega_r^{-1}D_r)^{-1}\). The feasible experiment
estimates these instrument maps on independent training trajectories.
Its sandwich estimates only a four-dimensional record-score covariance,
even when hundreds of weak moments supply the within-record model
weights.

The joint local information for \(\theta,u_1,\ldots,u_n\) has separate
nuisance blocks. Taking their Schur complement gives

\[
\mathcal I_{e,n}=\sum_r\mathcal I_e(u_r).
\]

Theorem 2 gives this sharp boundary-free reference record by record. For
fixed regular records, dense test spans recover it in the sequential
small-noise limit. Symmetric view separation preserves the leading
original-record influence when pilot instruments converge regularly,
because the auxiliary noises cancel at first order (S16.2). At finite
amplitude the full Hermite covariance and random instruments determine a
different sandwich. A simultaneous record-count, span-growth and
noise-decay rate theorem remains open.

\subsection{Covariance calibration and the common-trajectory
case}\label{covariance-calibration-and-the-common-trajectory-case}

The known-covariance construction isolates the initial-state nuisance
question. Estimated covariance requires a consistent calibration and its
influence contribution. Separate fixed-horizon residual fits estimate a
new initial state for every record; their residual quadratic forms can
retain incidental-parameter bias. Thus consistency of a residual
covariance estimate needs a bias correction or an independently
validated calibration scheme (S16.3). The pair-difference estimator in
S14 applies to equal means only.

If all records share one trajectory and covariance is known, their
Gaussian path average is sufficient for the mean parameter and has
covariance \(C/n\). Path averaging therefore provides the natural route
to Corollary 1. For nonlinear polynomial moments it also suppresses
higher Hermite orders faster than averaging separate moments. The
common-trajectory fixed-family GMM result is retained as Lemma 2 and a
calibration diagnostic in S14--S17.

\section{Discussion and
limitations}\label{discussion-and-limitations}

The central result is a statistical reference for boundary-free weak
equations when initial states are nuisance parameters. Their exact
adjoint range retains the nuisance-adjusted local information. Known
initial states make the excluded directions useful: the local RMSE cost
is 29\% for Van der Pol and almost a factor two for Lorenz. Rich test
spans recover most available weak information; correct temporal weights
add a smaller improvement. The full-path likelihood reference quantifies
the value of observations excluded by the boundary-free class.

Different initial states link the product information geometry to
conditional weak identities. Model covariance computes within-record
weights; independent instruments preserve those identities at fixed
noise, and the low-dimensional record score supplies its sandwich. A
common trajectory instead favors averaging the Gaussian paths. The
six-test common-trajectory experiment is consequently retained as a
calibration diagnostic. Its bootstrap at 50 records overcovers and
greatly expands the confidence region, motivating the richer
model-weighted heterogeneous-record experiment.

Local efficiency requires regular pilots, controlled quadrature and
compatible span enrichment. Moderate-noise performance depends
additionally on random-design and nonlinear terms; the matched WENDy
comparison leaves the accuracy ordering unresolved. Joint
record/span/noise rates, consistent covariance calibration with
heterogeneous fixed-horizon nuisance states, partial observation,
process noise and support selection need further analysis. The results
concern fully observed deterministic ODEs with supplied identifiable
structure, and provide theory and a reproducible benchmark for
evaluating weak estimators.
